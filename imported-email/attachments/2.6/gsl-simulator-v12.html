Precipitation Character Transition

The 1980 snow fraction baseline of 80.4% is declining at −0.18%/yr (FigD, corrected from prior 84% anchor). Below ~65–70% snow fraction, the dominant recharge mechanism shifts from sustained snowmelt hydrology to episodic pulse events with lower reservoir capture efficiency. This threshold has infrastructure implications: conveyance systems and reservoir operating rules designed for snowmelt become suboptimal for pulse-dominated hydrology. At the current rate, the threshold is reached approximately 2053–2058 absent acceleration. Policy interventions must address both volume delivered AND capture mode simultaneously.

Reframed Hypotheses — Statistical Validation Results

H1 — Structural Aridification Trend
GSL elevation is declining at a statistically significant rate driven by structural aridification.
SUPPORTED. Slope = −0.33 ft/yr (95% CI: −0.40 to −0.27), r = −0.839, p < 0.000001. Net loss 1977–2025: −16.3 ft. This is the bedrock finding of the paper and is publication-ready.
H2 — PDO as Decadal Regime Indicator
PDO phase governs multi-year GSL recharge capacity through modulation of the Aleutian Low and atmospheric river corridor.
SUPPORTED AT DECADAL SCALE, NULL AT ANNUAL. PDO–GSL annual r = −0.046 (p = 0.76, expected null — PDO is a regime indicator, not an annual driver). PDO–GSL 10-yr rolling r = +0.39 (p = 0.009). Phase lag: 6–8 years. Confirmed by Wang et al. 2010b. The null annual result is methodologically correct, not a failure.
H3 — ENSO Mediated by Atmospheric River Delivery
ENSO affects GSL through atmospheric river frequency and intensity, not annual SWE mean.
AR PATHWAY SUPPORTED; ANNUAL SWE CORRELATION NULL. El Niño years mean SWE = 105% of median; La Niña = 108% (t = −0.14, p = 0.89, null). Null result attributed to: (a) insufficient sample (n = 7 El Niño years), (b) ENSO signal operates through AR event intensity, not annual SWE average. A single nine-AR sequence (Dec 2022–Jan 2023) delivers more water than a full season of average snowpack. Chikamoto's AR frequency calibration required to close this.
H4 — PDO Suppression Paradox
The 2026 El Niño will produce a GSL response analogous to 2022–23.
REJECTED. Cool PDO (−4σ, historically anomalous) limits 2026 AR multiplier to ×1.8 vs. ×3.2 in 2022–23. The 2022–23 event benefited from a warm-transitioning PDO that sustained a 21-day subtropical moisture corridor (9 ARs). The 2026 analog is a meaningfully weaker recharge event. Stage 1 (precipitation): visible 2026–27. Stage 2 (GSL elevation): +3 yr lag, 2029–30.

Model Architecture — RC Circuit Analogy

The Great Salt Lake is modeled as a forced, damped oscillator analogous to an RC (resistor-capacitor) electrical circuit. External climate forcing (precipitation driven by ENSO×PDO×AR interactions) plays the role of a voltage source; the lake's hydrologic inertia plays the role of capacitance; and basin losses (evaporation, diversions) play the role of resistance. The lake charges during wet periods and discharges during dry periods with a characteristic time constant τ ≈ 9.3 years — the empirically derived lag between precipitation forcing and lake elevation response (Wang et al. 2010a, 2012).

GSL_elev(t) = GSL_baseline + A·[1 − exp(−t/τ)]·F_climate(t) − D_arid·t − W_evap(T)·t where: τ = 9.3 years (RC time constant; Wang & Gillies 2012) F_clim = ENSO_mult × PDO_amp × AR_mult × P_eff(snow_frac, T) D_arid = 0.33 ft/yr (structural aridification trend; H1, p < 0.000001) W_evap = warming evaporation penalty (+0.025 ft/yr/°C above baseline) snow_frac = 80.4% (1980 anchor, corrected) − 0.18%/yr (FigD) PDO: regime indicator at quasi-decadal scale (6–8 yr lag to GSL elevation). ENSO + AR: episodic step-changes at interannual scale; AR pathway primary. Stage 1 (precipitation response): immediate in water year of forcing. Stage 2 (GSL elevation response): +3 yr lag from precipitation anomaly.

ENSO × PDO × AR Interaction Framework

The Walker Circulation drives sea surface temperature anomalies across the tropical Pacific (ENSO). During El Niño, the weakened Walker Circulation shifts the Pacific jet stream southward, routing more atmospheric rivers (ARs) into the Southwest United States and Intermountain West. This teleconnection is strongly modulated by the Pacific Decadal Oscillation (PDO), which operates at quasi-decadal (8–25 year) periods. A warm PDO deepens the Aleutian Low, amplifying El Niño's southward jet displacement and sustaining the subtropical moisture corridor — producing AR multipliers of ×3.2 relative to climatology (as in 2022–23). A cool PDO suppresses this mechanism, limiting the multiplier to ×1.8 even during significant El Niño events (as projected for 2026).

The PDO's effect on GSL elevation operates with a 6–8 year lag (Wang et al. 2010b; Gillies et al. 2011), meaning PDO phase transitions do not immediately affect the lake. The current deeply Cool PDO (−4σ, historically anomalous as of 2026) will constrain GSL recharge through approximately 2031–2032, after which a neutral-to-warm transition may amplify the El Niño signal in subsequent events.

Precipitation Character Transition

A critical structural change in the water delivery pathway is the ongoing shift from snow-dominated to rain-dominated precipitation. The 1980 snow fraction baseline of 80.4% is declining at −0.18%/yr (FigD, corrected from erroneous prior 84% anchor). Below approximately 65–70% snow fraction, the dominant recharge mechanism shifts from sustained snowmelt — which slowly fills reservoirs over weeks to months — to episodic pulse events with lower capture efficiency. This transition has infrastructure implications: conveyance systems, reservoir operating rules, and water rights delivery schedules designed for snowmelt hydrology become suboptimal for pulse-dominated hydrology. Policy interventions must address both volume delivered AND capture mode simultaneously.

Known Model Limitations

  • Annual ENSO–SWE correlation is statistically null at current sample size (n = 7 El Niño years in record); the AR frequency coefficient M_AR requires calibration from Chikamoto's observational dataset.
  • PDO stationarity assumption: the model assumes PDO teleconnection structure is time-invariant. Anthropogenic warming may be altering ENSO/PDO characteristics (non-stationarity question; open for Chikamoto/Wang review).
  • Policy lever delivery rates are estimated; year-by-year actuals will vary with water year conditions (see Simulator tab accounting tables for conditional logic).
  • The structural aridification rate (−0.33 ft/yr) is a linear fit to 1977–2025 data; acceleration under continued warming is plausible but not explicitly modeled.
  • Evaporation penalty (+0.025 ft/yr/°C) is a first-order approximation; lake salinity effects on evaporation rate and the brine-shrimp ecosystem feedback are not explicitly modeled.
