# v14.1 Policy Lever Support Memo

Data vintage: 2026-05-28.

This companion memo documents the policy-lever ceilings and annual accounting profiles used in `gsl-simulator-v14.1.html`. It does not change simulator behavior. Its purpose is to make the simulator's policy-water assumptions reviewable by humans, future AI tools, and scientific/policy reviewers.

The key accounting distinction is:

```text
annual modeled policy water = lever ceiling * annual availability/profile fraction
```

The annual values are scenario assumptions, not claims that every year/lever value is independently observed or guaranteed. A ceiling may be well supported while annual availability remains episodic, conditional, or institutionally uncertain.

## Executive Accounting Guidance

1. Keep the simulator file clean. Do not embed this whole memo in the UI.
2. Keep annual accounting editable in the simulator, but document default profiles here.
3. Treat reductions in depletion as lake-balance equivalents, not literal new inflow.
4. Separate already-realized structural gains from new policy water.
5. Do not double-count US Magnesium-type gains if the forecast baseline already reflects reduced operations or state acquisition.
6. Keep augmentation outside the Strike Team policy-lever bibliography unless a specific augmentation project is being modeled.

## Lever Discussions

### Agricultural Optimization and Leasing

**Accounting category:** Wet-year dependent and participation-limited.

**Ceiling used:** 180 kAf/yr.

**Annual profile:** Variable, with full modeled availability only in 2030.

**Basis and rationale:** The 2026 Strike Team report states that agricultural water optimization and leasing can contribute meaningfully to lake recovery and identifies temporary water leasing as one of the most technically and economically viable strategies. It reports that several leasing scenarios evaluated in the Great Salt Lake Basin Integrated Plan were capable of achieving up to a 10% reduction in agricultural depletions if widely adopted.

The same report's updated water-budget table gives 2020-2024 agricultural depletion of 1,551 kAf/yr. A strict 10% calculation from that updated average is about 155 kAf/yr. The simulator's 180 kAf/yr ceiling is therefore an aggressive/high-end scenario, roughly consistent with 10% of a 1,700-1,800 kAf agricultural-depletion framing.

The USU depletion-methods report is essential because it distinguishes depletion reduction from diversion reduction. It defines irrigation depletion as the net removal of water from the hydrologic system after return flows and groundwater effects are considered, and it recommends practical methods for comparing depletion before and after irrigation practice changes. This source should be cited whenever simulator policy water is described as `depletion reduction` rather than simple diversion savings.

The CRA/CUWCD Upper Colorado agricultural conservation impact report provides quantitative support that a 180 kAf/yr ceiling is not outlandish in Utah agricultural settings. It estimates that temporary forage fallowing could save about 155.6 kAf/yr at 25% enrollment, 311.2 kAf/yr at 50% enrollment, and 466.7 kAf/yr at 75% enrollment. It also estimates crop-substitution savings near 143-148 kAf/yr and irrigation system conversion savings near 130 kAf/yr at the highest studied scale. That report is for Utah's Upper Colorado River Basin, not the Great Salt Lake Basin, so it supports order-of-magnitude plausibility and methods rather than a direct GSL ceiling.

The annual profile intentionally avoids treating the ceiling as a flat recurring amount. Agricultural leasing and optimization depend on water-year conditions, reservoir carryover, participation, compensation, conveyance ability, and water-right administration. Lower early years reflect ramp-up and limited participation. The 2028-2031 higher window reflects a scenario in which policy programs mature and favorable hydrologic/storage conditions allow more leasing.

**Review need:** Compare the 180 kAf/yr ceiling and annual fractions against the full Great Salt Lake Basin agricultural depletion-reduction opportunity report. If reviewers prefer a strict Strike-Team-derived ceiling, use about 155 kAf/yr based on 10% of the 2020-2024 agricultural depletion average.

### M&I Conservation

**Accounting category:** Stable recurring after adoption ramp.

**Ceiling used:** 100 kAf/yr.

**Annual profile:** 25%, 50%, 75%, then full recurring benefit from 2028 onward.

**Basis and rationale:** The 2026 Strike Team report updates human depletion accounting and finds M&I accounts for about 26.8% of human-caused depletions in 2020-2024. It also reports that outdoor water use accounts for nearly all M&I depletion in 2024, and residential lawn watering alone depleted 408.5 kAf.

The M&I Water Conservation Opportunities Report provides stronger ceiling support. It estimates 2019-2023 outdoor M&I depletion at 333 kAf/yr and states that outdoor depletion accounts for 96% of total M&I depletion. Its very-high-priority conservation table estimates turf replacement savings of 87-120 kAf/yr at 40% adoption and 131-180 kAf/yr at 60% adoption. A 100 kAf/yr ceiling is therefore defensible as a moderate-to-ambitious outdoor conservation scenario, especially if paired with turf replacement and irrigation-efficiency programs.

M&I conservation is not physically episodic in the same way as leasing or reservoir release. Once turf conversion, outdoor watering changes, ordinances, rates, and appliance/landscape changes take effect, the depletion reduction can persist. The profile therefore ramps over three years and then holds constant.

**Review need:** Verify whether 2028 full adoption is too aggressive. The ceiling is plausible from published outdoor M&I conservation estimates, but the adoption schedule is a policy implementation assumption.

### Phragmites Management

**Accounting category:** Buildout trajectory with maintenance.

**Ceiling used:** 50 kAf/yr.

**Annual profile:** Gradual ramp to full benefit by 2029.

**Basis and rationale:** The 2026 Strike Team report confirms phragmites management as a major ongoing effort and notes successful treatment results, including 11,000 acres treated around Great Salt Lake in 2024 and large cover reductions in many areas. It also explicitly states that additional research is needed to quantify water savings from treatment.

The M&I Water Conservation Opportunities Report cites Utah Division of Water Quality 2017 estimates that phragmites consumes 17-57 kAf/yr more water than native wetland vegetation, depending on density and site conditions. A 50 kAf/yr ceiling is within that published range, but because the Strike Team report flags remaining uncertainty, the simulator should keep this as an unverified or method-sensitive ceiling.

Phragmites treatment requires acreage access, herbicide or mechanical treatment, follow-up burning/cutting where appropriate, revegetation, and recurring maintenance. Benefits should not appear instantly. The profile assumes a 2025-2029 buildout and then sustained maintenance. If maintenance fails, recurring benefit should decline.

**Review need:** Confirm whether the 50 kAf/yr high-end estimate should be discounted for replacement vegetation, site-specific ET differences, and treatment persistence.

### Newfoundland Evaporation Basin

**Accounting category:** One-time or refillable stock transfer.

**Ceiling used:** 50 kAf/yr.

**Annual profile:** Front-loaded in 2025-2027, then zero without explicit refill.

**Basis and rationale:** This is one of the strongest directly supported lever ceilings. The 2026 Strike Team report states that wet-year surface water can exceed 100 kAf, dry years can be zero, median annual maximum storage is about 34 kAf, and practical diversion potential is estimated at 20-50 kAf/yr depending on hydrologic conditions. It also states that sustained delivery of this volume could increase long-term lake elevation by about 0.2-0.5 ft.

The HydroShare resource by Tarboton, Perry, Radwin, Abualqumboz, and Neilson reports the same practical 20-50 kAf/yr finding and describes the method: Landsat imagery, DEM analysis, evaporation estimates, and remote-sensing products over the prior 12 years.

This lever is best treated as stored or episodic water, not a recurring conservation program. If water is diverted out of the Newfoundland evaporation basin, that stock is spent unless later hydrology refills it. The current profile assumes an initial available stock spread over three years and no subsequent refill scenario.

**Review need:** Create an alternate profile for explicit 2026-2027 El Nino partial-refill assumptions if hydrologic evidence supports it.

### Mineral Extraction Reduction

**Accounting category:** Industrial depletion-reduction scenario.

**Ceiling used:** 150 kAf/yr in v14.1.

**Annual profile:** 50%, 75%, then full recurring benefit from 2027 onward.

**Basis and rationale:** The 2026 Strike Team report documents that mineral extraction policies changed significantly after 2022 and that reductions in mineral depletions have largely been voluntary. It reports that depletions from the three mineral companies declined from 186 kAf in 2020 to 77 kAf in 2024. The updated depletion table gives a 2020-2024 lake mineral extraction average of 136 kAf/yr.

These data justify tracking mineral extraction as a meaningful water-use category, but they do not by themselves justify a clean prospective recurring 150 kAf/yr policy ceiling. A 150 kAf/yr ceiling is best treated as an aggressive counterfactual or maximum scenario around historical depletion, not as a vetted deliverable reduction from current conditions.

The profile treats mineral extraction reduction as a structural industrial change that can persist after transition. This is the most sensitive ceiling/profile combination because recent reductions may already be partly embedded in the baseline.

**USMag self-check:** US Magnesium is not a v14.1 lever. It is displayed separately as structural baseline gain. Therefore the mineral slider should not also include USMag-type avoided depletion unless the scenario is explicitly using a pre-USMag counterfactual baseline. If the mineral slider is intended to represent additional current/future mineral-sector policy water beyond already-realized USMag protection, then the current 150 kAf/yr ceiling is too high.

**Recommended v14.2 adjustment:** reduce the mineral extraction slider ceiling to about 75-80 kAf/yr, matching the order of magnitude of reported 2024 remaining mineral depletion after the major recent reductions. Keep the 150 kAf/yr value only as an optional historical-counterfactual scenario, not as the default additive policy lever.

**Review need:** Verify current operator-specific depletions and separate already-realized reductions from new prospective water. Decide whether v14.2 should lower the public mineral lever to 80 kAf/yr while retaining a hidden/reference note that historical mineral depletion reached about 186 kAf/yr in 2020 and averaged about 136 kAf/yr during 2020-2024.

### US Magnesium-Type Avoided Depletion

**Accounting category:** Structural baseline gain, not new policy water and not a simulator lever.

**Number currently displayed in v14.1:** 65 kAf/yr.

**Related legal/right numbers:** Utah's acquisition of US Magnesium assets has been reported as including roughly 144,000-145,000 acre-feet of associated water rights. The Great Salt Lake Comprehensive Management Plan's older water-right table lists multiple US Magnesium rights that together are well above the 50-80 kAf/yr operational-use framing used in the simulator. Recent public reporting also states that US Magnesium pumped more than 52,000 acre-feet of lake brine and groundwater in 2024.

**Accounting answer:** US Magnesium-type avoided depletion should not be counted only in the first year. If the counterfactual is `US Magnesium continues withdrawing/depleting lake water every year`, then ending or acquiring those water rights creates an annual avoided-depletion benefit for as long as the water is legally and operationally protected for the lake. In the lake water-balance equation:

```text
change in storage = inflow - evaporation - withdrawals/depletions
```

Reducing a withdrawal by `X` kAf/yr improves lake storage by `X` kAf/yr, which is mathematically inflow-equivalent for lake-elevation modeling. It is not literally new tributary inflow; it is avoided loss. In a terminal lake budget, avoided depletion and added inflow have the same first-order sign and magnitude in storage accounting.

**Why v14.1 keeps it separate:** If the simulator forecast is anchored to current USGS elevation and current/post-cessation depletion conditions, then the US Magnesium gain may already be partly embedded in the baseline. Counting it again as slider-driven policy water would double-count it. For that reason, v14.1 displays US Magnesium-type gain as structural accounting context and excludes it from `modeledTotal`.

**Best accounting rule:** Treat US Magnesium as recurring avoided depletion in an historical/counterfactual budget, but as structural baseline context in the current forecast. Do not include it in new annual policy water unless the baseline explicitly assumes continued US Magnesium withdrawals. If USMag remains structurally protected, it should reduce the remaining mineral-sector lever ceiling rather than add to it.

**Review need:** Decide whether the 65 kAf/yr displayed value should be updated to one of three distinct quantities:

- `~52 kAf/yr`: recent actual 2024 pumping/use reported in public sources.
- `50-80 kAf/yr`: historical operational-use range used in prior simulator drafts.
- `~144-145 kAf/yr`: legal water-right volume acquired by the state, which should be treated as protected right/capacity rather than automatic annual hydrologic gain.

### Reservoir Operations and Releases

**Accounting category:** Episodic operational release.

**Ceiling used:** 180 kAf/yr.

**Annual profile:** Strongly variable.

**Basis and rationale:** The 2026 Strike Team report directly supports the 180 kAf/yr event-scale ceiling. It states that controlled releases of 80,000 acre-feet from Utah Lake delivered water to Great Salt Lake and that Weber Basin Water Conservancy District discharged over 100 kAf through Willard Spur into Bear River Bay. The simulator's 180 kAf/yr ceiling is the rounded sum of those two observed 2025 actions.

Repeated annual availability depends on storage, water rights, downstream needs, flood-control constraints, irrigation demand, and prior-year hydrology. The profile intentionally includes zero and low years because reservoir operations are opportunistic, not a guaranteed recurring supply.

**Review need:** Align annual fractions with a hydrologic trigger rule, such as reservoir carryover, snowpack percentile, streamflow forecast, or administrative release plan.

### New Water Augmentation

**Accounting category:** External augmentation stress test.

**Ceiling used in simulator:** 1,500 kAf/yr.

**Annual profile:** Construction-style ramp, but zero in the max-policy/zero-augmentation scenario.

**Basis and rationale:** The Strike Team report and Tarboton projections support sustained additional-inflow benchmarks of 250 kAf/yr and 800 kAf/yr, with 800 kAf/yr associated with a long-term mean lake level near 4,198 ft. The simulator's 1,500 kAf/yr slider ceiling goes beyond those published conservation/inflow benchmarks and functions as a stress-test or external augmentation scenario.

Augmentation is excluded from the documented max-policy scenario because the user specifically set augmentation to zero. The simulator keeps an augmentation ramp profile for other scenarios because large external supply projects have construction, permitting, funding, and conveyance delays.

**Review need:** Keep augmentation documentation separate from Strike Team conservation-lever documentation unless a specific augmentation project, source volume, conveyance plan, cost, and schedule are cited.
